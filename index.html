What the words mean

Five terms carry everything here. None is a judgement about you.

an entry
One line you wrote, and the moment you wrote it. Rocky keeps the line for you to read later. It does not read the line itself.
quiet
The time between one entry and the next. Rocky treats quiet as information rather than as missing data, because a long quiet is the thing worth noticing.
your usual gap
The average quiet across your own record. Someone who writes twice a day and someone who writes twice a month each have their own usual, and neither is compared to the other.
the multiple, written 3.4x
How long the current quiet has run, divided by your usual gap. At 3.4x you have been quiet three and a half times longer than you normally are. It is a ratio and nothing else. It is not a score, a risk level, or a measure of how you are doing.
what you decided
A sentence you write in advance, kept word for word, and handed back when the condition you chose is met. Rocky holds the sentence. It does not write one.

What it will never do

Each of these is a way of being confidently wrong about a person, so each is refused in the design rather than in a policy document. This is a list of limits, not a list of features.

  • Know how you feel. It has no way to tell, and does not guess.
  • Read what you write. Your words are kept for you. The only thing taken from them is a count of how many people you named.
  • Compare you to anyone. Not to other founders, not to a norm, not to a population. Nothing here knows what anyone else does.
  • Diagnose, score, or rate you.
  • Detect a crisis. It cannot, and does not try.
  • Decide anything you did not decide first.
  • Send anything anywhere. It has no network access at all.
  • Talk to you. It has no voice and no opinions. It hands back your own words at the moment you asked for them, then stops.

What this borrows, and what it is not

None of the ideas here are original. Three bodies of work are worth knowing about, both for what they support and for where they stop.

  • Deciding in advance, in if-then form. Linking a specific cue to a specific response is the most studied version of this. A meta-analysis of 94 tests found a medium to large effect on whether the intended thing actually happens, and a 2024 analysis across 642 tests found effects across cognitive, affective and behavioural outcomes. Two things made the effect larger: contingent if-then phrasing, and rehearsing the plan. That is why the sentence here is shaped as a condition and a response, and why it is shown back to you on ordinary days rather than only on hard ones.
  • Naming people in advance. Deciding who to contact before you need to is one component of safety planning, an approach with solid evidence in clinical suicide prevention. Rocky is not a safety plan and its evidence does not transfer here. A real safety plan is made with a clinician, covers warning signs, coping steps and access to means, and is for people at risk. Rocky does none of that and cannot detect risk at all. If a safety plan is what you need, ask a clinician for one rather than using this.
  • Writing things down. Writing about experiences that matter has been studied for decades with small to moderate benefits. Those studies use long sessions of reflective writing. A one line entry here is not that, so treat the record as a record and not as a treatment.

Rocky has not been studied. What is described above is evidence for the ideas it borrows, which is a different claim, and a weaker one.

Why one number, and what was tested and rejected

Five methods were built and tested on simulated records, scored causally, each compared against firing at random at the same volume. Precision is how often an alert landed inside a genuinely hard stretch. The numbers are reported as they came out.

The simplest method has the highest precision in that table. The four more elaborate ones added nothing measurable over it, so none of them is in this page. The fixed eleven day rule scored below chance. All of it is simulated people rather than real ones, which limits what any of it shows.

Two questions remain open. Whether a reminder to tell someone makes a person more likely to tell them, or quietly satisfies the urge instead. And that the moment Rocky sounds, it changes what it was listening to. Both need a study rather than more code, and neither has been run.
