A list of limits, not of features.

None of the ideas here are original. Three are worth knowing, for what they support and where they stop.

  • Deciding in advance, in if-then form. Well studied, with a medium to large effect on whether the intended thing happens. Effects were larger with if-then phrasing and with rehearsal, which is why the sentence has this shape and is shown back on ordinary days.
  • Naming people in advance. One component of safety planning, which has solid clinical evidence. Rocky is not a safety plan and that evidence does not transfer. A safety plan is made with a clinician and covers warning signs and access to means. If that is what you need, ask a clinician for one rather than using this.
  • Writing things down. Studied for decades, small to moderate benefits, using long reflective sessions. A one line entry is not that, so treat this as a record and not as a treatment.

Rocky has not been studied. The above is evidence for the ideas it borrows, which is a weaker claim.
